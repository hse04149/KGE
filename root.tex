\begin{abstract}                % Abstract of 50--100 words
This paper studies formation control of single-integrator nodes. Inspired by knowledge graph embedding, we formulate displacement-based formation control within a unified formation graph embedding (FGE) framework and show that existing control laws can be derived using distributed optimization techniques. We define the feasibility of FGEs based on whether the desired formation constraints are realizable and analyze the system's behavior when displacement-based FGE is infeasible. Our theoretical results identify conditions for specific motion patterns under infeasibility, which are validated through numerical simulations. This study provides a unified perspective on formation control and enhances understanding of multi-agent coordination under infeasibility.
\end{abstract}

Formation control, graph embedding, formation configuration, displacement-based control.

\section{Introduction}

% Over the past two decades, formation control has been a subject of extensive research due to its theoretical significance and diverse practical applications. It involves coordinating multiple mobile agents, such as robots, drones, or autonomous vehicles, to maintain a prescribed geometric configuration while navigating or executing tasks. The fundamental challenge is to regulate the positions and orientations of agents using only local sensing and communication mechanisms.

% As highlighted in \cite{oh2015survey}, formation control strategies can be broadly categorized into displacement-based and distance-based approaches. The displacement-based method relies on controlling the relative positions of neighboring agents. In contrast, the distance-based approach ensures that agents preserve predefined inter-agent distances using only local measurements, which often results in a more decentralized control structure.

Displacement-based formation control has been extensively studied due to its advantages in balancing sensing requirements and communication constraints. Unlike distance-based control, displacement-based control enables agents to regulate their relative positions with respect to their local coordinate frames while assuming orientation alignment across the agents. This approach eliminates the need for global positioning systems and simplifies the implementation of distributed formation control. Furthermore, it ensures robustness to environmental disturbances and communication failures while providing a moderate level of decentralization compared to position- and distance-based approaches \citep{oh2015survey}.

Due to these advantages, a significant amount of research has focused on displacement-based formation control. Theoretical developments have analyzed its application to various agent dynamics, including single-integrator \citep{jadbabaie2003coordination, ren2005consensus}, double-integrator \citep{ren2007distributed}, and general linear systems \citep{fax2004information}. The feasibility of achieving desired formations has been studied using graph-theoretic methods, particularly in terms of connectedness and the existence of spanning trees \citep{ren2005consensus}. Stability analysis has been conducted for both static and time-varying interaction graphs, demonstrating that uniform connectedness is a sufficient condition for ensuring asymptotic convergence \citep{moreau2005stability, lin2007state}. Additionally, practical issues such as maintaining connectivity under limited sensing ranges \citep{ji2007distributed} and ensuring formation transitions \citep{ren2007distributed} have been addressed.
% Extensions to nonholonomic agents, such as unicycles, have also been explored, showing that desired formations can be achieved despite kinematic constraints \citep{dimarogonas2003decentralized}.

While displacement-based formation control has been extensively studied, most existing research has focused on cases where the constraints on relative positions required to achieve a given formation are feasible. In contrast, studies addressing the behavior of the system when these constraints are infeasible have been limited to a few works, such as \cite{dimarogonas2007further, dimarogonas2008connection}. Motivated by this gap, this paper investigates the effects of infeasibility in displacement-based formation control, specifically examining the system's behavior when the relative position constraints defining the desired formation cannot be satisfied.

The main contributions of this paper are twofold. First, inspired by knowledge graph embedding, we formulate formation control problems within the framework of graph embedding. In particular, we introduce the concept of formation graph embedding to represent formation control problems and define the feasibility of a formation graph embedding based on whether the formation constraints can be satisfied. Second, we analyze the behavior of the formation control system when the formation graph embedding is infeasible. Specifically, we theoretically establish conditions under which the node velocities converge to a common value and conditions under which the nodes converge to stationary points. While \cite{dimarogonas2007further, dimarogonas2008connection} addressed the issue of infeasibility under the assumption that the graph is balanced, we consider a more general setting in this paper. Furthermore, unlike \cite{dimarogonas2007further, dimarogonas2008connection}, we demonstrate that the nodes can maintain their relative positions while moving at a common velocity. Finally, numerical simulations validate the theoretical findings and confirm the accuracy of our analysis.

\subsection{Algebraic graph theory}

A directed graph (or graph) ${G} = ({V}, {E}, A)$ is defined as a triple consisting of a node set $V = \{ 1, \ldots, N \}$, an edge set ${E} \subset \{ (i, j): i, j \in V, i \not =  j \}$, where an edge is an ordered pair of distinct nodes in $V$, and a weight matrix $A = [a_{ij}] \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$, which is a nonnegative matrix such that $a_{ij} > 0$ if and only if $(j,i) \in E$. The weight matrix is called the adjacency matrix of $G$. 
% If $a_{ij} = a_{ji}$ for all $(j,i) \in E$, $G$ is called undirected. 
If $(i, j)$ is an edge of $G$, $i$ is a parent node of $j$ and $j$ is a child node of $i$. A root is a node that has no parent. The set of neighbors of node $i$ in $G$ is defined as $\mathcal{N}_{i} = \{ j \in V: (j,i) \in E \}$. % The in-valency of node $i$ is the number of nodes in $\mathcal{N}_{i}$.

If $V_{s} = V$, $G_{s}$ is called a spanning subgraph of $G$. If $E_{s}$ consists of all of the edges in $E$ that have both endpoints in $V_{s}$, $G_{s}$ is called an induced subgraph of $G$.

A tree is a graph such that every node except for a node called the root of the tree has exactly one parent.

\subsection{Knowledge graph embedding}

A knowledge graph $\mathcal{K} = (\mathcal{E},\mathcal{R})$ is defined as a pair consisting of a set of entities $\mathcal{E}$ and a set of relations $\mathcal{R}$. 
A knowledge graph stores manifold facts (or knowledges) as a collection of triples $\mathcal{F}=\{(h,r,t)\}$, where $h \in \mathcal{E}$ and $t  \in \mathcal{E}$ are referred to as the head and tail entities, respectively, and $r$ denotes the relation connecting them. 

For a knowledge graph $\mathcal{K} = (\mathcal{E},\mathcal{R})$, graph embedding is an optimization task aimed at finding low-dimensional representations of a knowledge graph’s entities and relations while preserving their semantic meaning. For a fact triple $(h,r,t)$, let the embedding vectors of $h$, $r$, and $t$  in $\mathbb{R}^d$ by $\mathbf{h}$, $\mathbf{r}$, and $\mathbf{t}$, respectively. The knowledge graph embedding problem is then defined as follows:
\begin{align} \label{eq_kge}
\textrm{minimize} \sum_{(h,r,t) \in \mathcal{F}} f(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{r},\mathbf{t}),
\end{align}
where $f$ is a function that encodes the relations. Roughly speaking, the goal is to find $\mathbf{h}$, $\mathbf{r}$, and $\mathbf{t}$ such that $f( \mathbf{h}, \mathbf{t}, \mathbf{r}) \approx 0$. Depending on the choice of the function $f$, various knowledge graph embedding models can be fomulated \citep{wang_knowledge_2017, cao2024knowledge}. For instance, setting $f(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{t},\mathbf{r}) = \mathbf{h} -\mathbf{t} - \mathbf{r}$, the following model can be formulated:
\begin{align} \label{eq_transe}
\textrm{minimize} \sum_{(h,r,t) \in {\mathcal{F}}} \| \mathbf{t}-\mathbf{h} - \mathbf{r} \|^2,
\end{align}
which is known as TransE \citep{bordes_translating_2013}. Details on knowledge graph embedding can be found in \cite{wang_knowledge_2017, cao2024knowledge}.

For a group of single-integrator agents, a displacement-based formation control problem can be formulated as follows. Consider the following $N$ single-integrator agents:
\begin{align} \label{eq_single_integrator}
\dot{x}_i = u_i,
\end{align}
where $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and $u_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ denote the position and control input of agent $i$, for $i = 1,\ldots,N$.  Assume that each agent is able to sense the relative positions (displacements) of its neighboring agents. Specifically, we assume that agent $i$ senses $x_i - x_j$ if agent $j$ is one of its neighboring agents. The sensing topology can be modeled as a graph, $G = (V,E,A)$, where the nodes denote the agents and an edge $e_{ji} \in E$ indicates that agent $i$ senses $x_i - x_j$. The desired formation can be speficified by the desired displacement $r_{j,i} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ for each $(j,i) \in E$. Then the displacement-based formation control problem is state as follows: 
\begin{problem} \label{problem_displacement}
For the $N$ agents modeled as \eqref{eq_single_integrator}, the relative position sensing topology is given by $G = (V,E,A)$. Design the control law $u_i$ such that the condition $x_i - x_j = r_{j,i}$ for  all $(i,j) \in E$.
\end{problem}

\subsection{Solution of FGEs via distributed optimization}
We solve the FGEs correspond to the formation control problems in \textit{Problems \ref{problem_displacement}} based on distributed optimization. We first consider the displacement-based formation control problem. The FGE \eqref{eq_transe_formation} can be arranged as
\begin{align*}
& \textrm{minimize} \sum_{i \in V}f_i(x_i,x_j,r_{j,i}), \\
 & f_i(x_i,x_j,r_{j,i}) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} \| x_i - x_j - {r_{j,i}} \|^2.
\end{align*}
That is, the FGE \eqref{eq_transe_formation} can be solved by optimizing the local objective functions. The update rule for $x_i$ is then designed as the following gradient descent law:
\begin{align*}
\dot{x}_i &= - \nabla_{x_i} f_i (x_i , x_j,r_{j,i}) \\
               &= -\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} (x_i - x_j - r_{j,i}),
\end{align*}
which can be written in the vector form as
\begin{align} \label{eq_displacement_based_law}
\dot{x} = -(L \otimes I_{d} x-r),
\end{align}
where $r = [r_1 \; r_2 \; \cdots \: r_N]^T$ with $r_i = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i} r_{j,i}$. Notice that the equation \eqref{eq_displacement_based_law} corresponds to the well-known displacement-based formtaion control law \citep{olfati-saber2004consensus,ren2005consensus,lin2007state,dimarogonas2008connection,oh2015survey}. In short, \textit{Problem \ref{problem_displacement}} can be viewed as a FGE and a distributed optimization based update rule for the problem corresponds to the well-known displacement-based formation control law.

\subsection{Feasiblity of FGEs}
 As discussed, formation control problems can be formulated as FGEs. To further investigate the behavior of the formation control system \eqref{eq_displacement_based_law}, we define the following notion of feasibility of FGEs:
 \begin{definition} [\textbf{Feasibility of FGEs}]
An FGE $(G,\mathcal{F},d,f)$ is feasible if there exist $x_1^*,\ldots,x_N^*$, where $N$ denotes the number of nodes of $G$, such that 
\begin{align*}
\sum_{(i,r_{j,i},j) \in \mathcal{F}}f(x^*_i,x_j^*,r_{j,i}) = 0.
\end{align*}
If not, the FGE $(G,\mathcal{F},d,f)$ is infeasible.
 \end{definition}

By definition, the FGE \eqref{eq_transe_formation} is feasible if there exists a constant vector $x^*$ such that $(L \otimes I_d)x^* = r$. Otherwise it is infeasible. If the FGE \eqref{eq_transe_formation} is feasible, the dispalcement-based formation control law \eqref{eq_displacement_based_law} can be reduced into a consensus law. To see this, define $\tilde{x} = x - x^*$. Then \eqref{eq_displacement_based_law} can be arranged as follows:
 \begin{align} \label{eq_consensus}
 \dot{\tilde{x}} = - (L \otimes I_d) \tilde{x},
 \end{align}
 which correspond to the well-known consensus law for single-integrator agents \cite{olfati-saber2004consensus,ren2005consensus,lin2007state,dimarogonas2008connection,oh2015survey}. The behavior of the system described by \eqref{eq_consensus} has been widely investigated \cite{olfati-saber2004consensus,ren2005consensus,lin2007state,oh2015survey} assuming the feasibility of the FGE. For instance, if the sensing graph $G$ has a spanning tree, $\tilde{x} \to 0$ as $t \to \infty$

While the FGE \eqref{eq_transe_formation} has been widely studied in the literature, majority of the existing results have focused on feasible FGEs. Though infeasible FGEs have been studied in \cite{dimarogonas2008connection}, there remain further researches to be conducted. Accordingly, we focus on the behavior of the displacement-based formation control system \eqref{eq_displacement_based_law} under the assumption that the corresponding FGE is infeasible.


\section{Infeasible FGEs} \label{sec_infeasible}

In this section, we examine the displacement-based formation control system \eqref{eq_displacement_based_law} under the assumption that the corresponding FGE is infeasible. For clarity, we consider the case where $ d = 1 $. That is, we consider the formation graph embedding $(G,\mathcal{F},d,f)$, where $d=1$ and $f(x_i,x_j) = \frac{1}{2}\| x_j-x_i-r_{j,i} \|^2$. The corresponding formation control system is given by 
\begin{align} \label{eq_displacement_1d}
\dot{x} = -(Lx - r), \; x(0) = x_0,
\end{align}
where $x \in \mathbb{R}^N$. Though we assume that the embedding dimension $d$ is one, the results presented in this section can be readily generalized to higher dimensions.

To analyze \eqref{eq_displacement_1d} when the corresponding FGE $(G,\mathcal{F},d,f)$ is infeasible, we first investigate the limit of $\dot{x}(t)$ as $t \to \infty$.
\begin{lemma} \label{lemma_1}
For the displacement-based formation control system \eqref{eq_displacement_1d}, let $m$ be the multiplicity of the zero eigenvalue of the Laplacian matrix $L$ of the sensing graph $G$. Let $p_k$ and $q_k$ be the right and left eigenvectors associatedwith the $k$-th zero eigenvalue for $k = 1,\ldots,m$. Then,
%\begin{align*}
%    \lim_{t\rightarrow \infty} \dot{x}(t) = \sum_{k=1}^m p_k q_k^\top \dot{x}(0).
%\end{align*}
\begin{align*}
    \lim_{t\rightarrow \infty} \dot{x}(t) = \sum_{k=1}^m p_k q_k^\top r.
\end{align*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{pf}
Differentiating the both sides of \eqref{eq_displacement_1d}, we obtain $\ddot{x}(t)=-L\dot{x}(t)$, solution of which is given by $\dot{x}(t)=e^{-Lt}\dot{x}(0)$. By the Jordan decomposition theorem, there exists an invertible matrix $T$ such that $L = TJT^{-1}$, where $J$ is the Jordan form of $L$. From \textit{Lemma \ref{lem_zero_eigen_multiplicity}}, it follows that $J$ contains exactly $m$ Jordan blocks of size $1 \times 1$ associated with the zero eigenvalue.
Using Jordan decomposition,
\begin{align*}
    \dot{x}(t) = e^{-Lt}\dot{x}(0) = Te^{-Jt}T^{-1}\dot{x}(0).
\end{align*}
It follows from \textit{Lemma \ref{lemma_Laplacian_1}} that all nonzero eigenvalues of $L$ have positive real parts. Thus, for any nonzero eigenvalue $ \lambda_i $ of $ L $, $ e^{- \lambda_i t} \to 0 $ as $ t \to \infty $. It follows that
\begin{align*}
    \lim_{t\rightarrow \infty} \dot{x} &= \sum_{k=1}^m p_k q_k^\top \dot{x}(0) = \sum_{k=1}^m p_k q_k^\top (r-Lx_0)  = \sum_{k=1}^m p_k q_k^\top r,
\end{align*}
which completes the proof. $\blacksquare$
\end{pf}
If the FGE is feasible, then there exists $x^*$ such that $Lx^* = r$.
According to \textit{Lemma \ref{lemma_1}}, if the FGE is feasible, then we have
\begin{align*}
    \lim_{t\rightarrow \infty} \dot{x}(t) = \sum_{k=1}^m p_k q_k^\top r = \sum_{k=1}^m p_k q_k^\top Lx^* = 0,
\end{align*}
which shows that the nodes converge to stationary points. If the FGE is infeasible, the convergence property depends on the sensing graph $G$, as will be shown in below.

Based on \textit{Lemma \ref{lemma_1}}, we present the following theorem, which is about the behavior of the nodes when $G$ is strongly connected and the FGE is infeasible.
\begin{theorem} \label{thm_main}
For the displacement-based formation control system \eqref{eq_displacement_1d}, assume the following:
\begin{itemize}
\item $G$ is strongly connected.
\item The FGE is infeasible.
\end{itemize}
Let $p_1 = \mathbf{1}_N$ and $q_1$ be the right and left eigenvectors associated with the zero eigenvalue such that $q_1^\top p_1 = 1$. Then the following is true: 
\begin{align*}
\lim_{t\rightarrow \infty} \dot{x}(t) &=  q_1^\top r \mathbf{1}_N, \\
\lim_{t \to \infty} Lx(t) &= r - q_1^\top r  \mathbf{1}_N.
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}
\begin{pf}
Since $G$ is strongly connected, it follows from \textit{Lemma \ref{lemma_Laplacian_2}} that zero is a simple eigenvalue associated with the eigenvector $\mathbf{1}_N$. It follows from \textit{Lemma \ref{lemma_1}} that 
\begin{align*}
\lim_{t\rightarrow \infty} \dot{x}(t) &= p_1 q_1^\top r = q_1^\top r  \mathbf{1}_N.
\end{align*}
Further, from \eqref{eq_displacement_1d}, we obtain
\begin{align*}
\lim_{t \to \infty} \dot{x}(t) = q_1^\top r  \mathbf{1}_N = \lim_{t\to \infty}(r-Lx(t)),
\end{align*}
which completes the proof. $\blacksquare$
\end{pf}

\textit{Theorem \ref{thm_main}} shows that if $G$ is stronlgy connected and the FGE is infeasible, the velocities of the nodes converge to an identical value, which can be determined by $G$ and $r$. Nonetherless, according to \textit{Theorem \ref{thm_main}}, the relative positions of the nodes satisfy the constraint, $\lim_{t \to \infty} Lx(t) = r - q_1^\top r  \mathbf{1}_N$. Note that \textit{Theorem \ref{thm_main}} can be applied to a root-like induced subgraph that has an infeasible corresponding FGE.





Finally, the following theorem states a condition under which the nodes converge to stationary points even when the FGE is infeasible.
\begin{theorem} \label{thm_main_2}
For the displacement-based formation control system \eqref{eq_displacement_1d}, assume the following:
\begin{itemize}
\item $G$ has a spanning tree that includes one root.
\item There exists no root-like induced subgraph in $G$.
\item The FGE is infeasible.
\end{itemize}
Then the nodes converge to stationary points.
\end{theorem}
%
%
\begin{pf}
Since $G$ has a spanning tree, it follows from \textit{Lemma \ref{lemma_Laplacian_2}} that zero is a simple eigenvalue associated with the eigenvector $\mathbf{1}_N$. It follows from \textit{Lemma \ref{lemma_1}} that 
\begin{align*}
\lim_{t\rightarrow \infty} \dot{x}(t) &= p_1 q_1^\top r = q_1^\top r  \mathbf{1}_N.
\end{align*}
This shows that the velocities of all the nodes converge to an identical value. Without loss of generality, we assume that node $1$ is a root. It receives no external forces, then $\dot{x}_1 = 0$, which shows that all the nodes converge to stationary points. 
$\blacksquare$
\end{pf}

\begin{remark}
Several remarks are in order here:
\begin{itemize}
\item As demonstrated in \textit{Theorem \ref{thm_main}}, strong connectivity appears to contribute to the undesirable phenomenon in which the nodes move at an identical speed when the FGE is infeasible. We conjecture that, even when the FGE is infeasible, the nodes may instead converge to stationary points if no root-like induced subgraph exists.

\item Although \textit{Theorem \ref{thm_main_2}} assumes that $G$ has a spanning tree, we conjecture that a similar result can be established even when $G$ constains multiple root nodes, provided that no root-like induced subgraph exsits in $G$. 

\item Finally, we aim to extend the results presented in this paper to the more general case where $G$ is weakly connected.
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\section{Conclusion}	\label{sec_conclusion}
In this paper, we investigated formation control problem for single-integrator nodes. 
% Motivated by knowledge graph embedding, we first formulated the displacement- and distance-based formation control problems in a unified manner using the framework of the formation graph embedding. 
Motivated by knowledge graph embedding, we first formulated the displacement-based formation control problems in a unified manner using the framework of the formation graph embedding. 
% Furthermore, we demonstrated that the well-established displacement- and distance-based formation control laws in existing literature can be derived through the application of distributed optimization techniques to the FGE.
Furthermore, we demonstrated that the well-established displacement-based formation control laws in existing literature can be derived through the application of distributed optimization techniques to the FGE.
Subsequently, we defined the feasibility of the FGE based on whether the constraints determining the desired formation shape are realizable. In particular, we analyzed the behavior of nodes when the FGE is infeasible. As a direction for future work, we aim to extend the results of this paper to more general cases where the sensing graph is a weakly connected graph.
